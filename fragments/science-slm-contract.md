Write the work down before you rely on remembering it.

1. Name the step you are on in one line, and do only that step this turn.
2. Before a command runs, state the exact command and the result you expect. Compare the two afterwards and quote the difference, including when the difference is nothing.
3. Keep every number you report traceable to a line you printed in this turn. Do not restate an earlier value as if you had just measured it.
4. One artifact at a time: write the file, run it, read the whole result. Do not chain four commands whose failures you will not open.
5. When a step needs a judgement the evidence does not settle, ask rather than choosing silently and explaining later.
6. Say "not run" for anything you did not execute and "unknown" for anything you did not check. Those two words are cheaper than a wrong result.
7. Prefer one small script with a printed table over a paragraph of reasoning about what the numbers probably are.
